Coprinellus disseminatus: Biological & Industrial Synthesis

Coprinellus disseminatus (the trooping crumble cap) represents an unusual evolutionary node within the Psathyrellaceae family. Lacking the autolytic deliquescence characteristic of most coprinoid species, it combines persistent non-inky fruiting bodies with high-throughput saprotrophic enzymes, a simplified bipolar mating system, and valuable pharmacological compounds. Coprinellus disseminatus (Pers.) J.E. Lange

Trooping Habit
Macroscopic Morphology:

Pileus 0.5–1.5 cm, campanulate, ash-gray pleated surface. Non-deliquescent gills turn dark brown/black. Stem thin, white, hollow (up to 4 cm). Fruiting troops in massive swarms on decaying hardwood logs.

Micro-Diagnostic Characteristics:

Basidiospores 7.0–9.5 × 4.0–5.0 µm, smooth almond-shaped with central apical germ pore. Q-ratio 1.75–1.9. 4-spored basidia, epithelium pileipellis, clamp connections abundant.

Taxonomic Shift Rationale: Molecular multigene phylogenetics proved that the traditional genus Coprinus was highly polyphyletic. Coprinus comatus remained in Agaricaceae, while coprinoid lineages were transferred to Coprinellus, Coprinopsis, and Parasola within the family Psathyrellaceae.
